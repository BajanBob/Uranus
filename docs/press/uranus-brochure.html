A free 2 m beacon mode that decodes far below the noise. Testers wanted worldwide.

Uranus is free beacon software for the 2 m and 70 cm amateur bands. Its new coherent mode, URC, decodes a one-minute transmission at 30.8 dB below the noise in a 2500 Hz bandwidth, with no prior knowledge of the caller. Now it needs stations on the air.

BRIDGETOWN, BARBADOS — 8 November 2026. The Uranus project, led by Kammie Holder, 8P5AB, today released version 0.2.0 of its free beacon software for the 2 m and 70 cm amateur radio bands. The release introduces URC, a coherent weak-signal mode that recovers its carrier from known pilot symbols and demodulates it coherently.

In full audio testing, where the decoder must find the signal's timing and frequency on its own, URC-60 decoded at −30.8 dB and URC-15 at −24.7 dB. That is 2.7 to 4.0 dB better than WSJT-X's FST4 at the same slot length and the same transmitter peak power.

That puts URC ahead of the published FT8, FST4 and Q65 figures for the same slot length, whenever the receiving station has no prior knowledge of the caller.

Uranus is freeware, and the project's next step depends on other amateurs. Every result so far comes from simulation and audio testing. The team is inviting stations around the world to put URC on the air, report what they decode and help turn laboratory numbers into field results.

The design rules came out of the measurements. An early filtered-BPSK version lost 3.2 dB to its peak-to-average ratio. The team switched to MSK, which keeps a constant envelope, so a transceiver runs it at full power with no loss of sensitivity.

Uranus runs on a Raspberry Pi or a Windows PC with any Hamlib-controlled all-mode rig, and also sequences APRS, FT8 and WSPR beacons.

“We set out to decode below −30 dB in under a minute, and in testing Uranus did it on a 2-metre beacon here in Barbados. Uranus is free for everyone. Now we need stations on the air to prove it, and we invite amateurs around the world to join our on-air tests.”

Kammie Holder, 8P5AB · Uranus project lead

Five design choices behind URC

Each one was kept because it measured better, not because it sounded good.

01
Choice

Trade speed for sensitivity.

What it gives

A ladder of submodes from 15 s to 300 s. Every halving of rate gains about 3 dB, and URC-60 occupies under 10 Hz.

02
Choice

A built-in carrier reference.

What it gives

One known pilot in every five symbols, tracked by a Kalman filter and smoother that also uses the pilots that come after each symbol.

03
Choice

Coherent detection at full transmitter power.

What it gives

Coherent MSK: BPSK sensitivity with a constant envelope (0.0 dB peak-to-average, measured).

04
Choice

Strong error correction.

What it gives

A K=9, rate ⅓ convolutional code with a soft-decision Viterbi decoder, plus a CRC-16 to reject false decodes.

05
Choice

Precise frequency at both ends.

What it gives

GPS-disciplined oscillators, so the decoder searches only ±0.6 Hz for URC-60.

The numbers

Signal strength needed for 50 % of transmissions to decode, SNR in 2500 Hz (the WSJT-X convention). Longer bars mean weaker signals can be decoded.

What's in v0.2.0

Band and mode sequencer

2 m APRS, FT8, WSPR and URC, a 3-minute pause, then 70 cm. Each slot is aligned to its UTC boundary, with 1 minute between transmissions.

URC coherent mode

Pilot-aided carrier recovery, constant-envelope MSK and a soft Viterbi decoder. Five submodes from 13 s to 4.4 min on air.

UR noncoherent mode

4-GFSK with a priori decoding and repeat stacking, for stations without a GPSDO.

Link-budget tool

A P/N0 link budget for your station that shows the margin in every mode before you go on air.

Listen before transmit

APRS waits for a clear channel using the rig's S-meter, so the beacon does not transmit over other stations.

Uranus.exe for Windows

A double-click control window, no Python required. It also runs on a Raspberry Pi with any Hamlib-controlled rig.

Testers wanted, worldwide

Uranus is free. The only price is a report of what you heard. Any licensed amateur, anywhere, can take part.

What you need

A 2 m or 70 cm all-mode rig with CAT control and USB audio, and a PC or Raspberry Pi. For URC, a GPS-disciplined oscillator; without one, run UR, FT8 and WSPR.

What to do

Transmit or listen for URC-60 alongside FST4W-120 or Q65-60A on the same path, so the modes can be compared like for like.

How to report

Open an issue on the GitHub page with your callsign, grid, path, the decode SNR of each mode and any recordings. Every report shapes the next release.

About these results

  • Not yet tested on the air. All thresholds come from simulation and audio loopback in white Gaussian noise. On-air trials between GPSDO-equipped stations are the next milestone.
  • URC-120 and URC-300 figures (−34.1 and −38.1 dB) are simulation results; URC-15 and URC-60 were measured through the full audio decoder.
  • WSJT-X thresholds are the published figures from the FST4 and Q65 quick-start guides. WSJT-X is the work of K1JT and the WSJT development group.
  • Operators must check their licence conditions for new digital modes, unattended operation and every frequency they enable.
